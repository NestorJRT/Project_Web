Sustainable Development Goal 03

Nappy: small habits,
sustainable well-being.

A web application that helps students track daily sleep, hydration, physical activity and mood - then turns that information into small, realistic actions.

Project proposal - revised Group 6 - Nestor Ramirez and Alvaro Perez September 2026

01 · Introduction

Health is built every day

The 2030 Agenda brings together 17 goals to improve lives and protect the planet. SDG 3 promotes healthy lives and well-being for everyone.

Health is also built through daily habits: sleeping, staying hydrated, moving and recognizing our emotions. University life makes these habits difficult to maintain, so Nappy makes the first step simple and useful.

Our promise: Nappy does not diagnose or replace health professionals. It supports habit-building, gives practical guidance and points users toward professional resources when needed.

The product is designed for reflection and prevention, not medical diagnosis.

02 · Rationale

Why did we choose SDG 3?

We chose this goal because it is close to our community, and a website can help without special equipment. A few data points can reveal patterns and make it easier to start with one manageable change.

Personal relevance

Students directly face challenges involving sleep, stress and sedentary routines.

Inclusive reach

A responsive website works on phones, tablets and computers, with an accessible, low-friction experience.

Technical learning

The project practices forms, data persistence, visualization and validation in one coherent product.

Measurable impact

We can measure registered days, completed actions, return rate and whether users feel the app was useful.

03 · Problem

Scattered information, blind decisions

Many people want to take care of themselves but do not connect their habits with their energy and mood. Existing apps can be complex or split by habit, which becomes discouraging.

Guiding question

How can we help students track and understand essential well-being habits without making self-care feel overwhelming?

Initial users

Students aged 18 to 30 who have little time but want to improve their routines with small steps.

Design insight: the problem is not only missing information. It is the gap between noticing a pattern and knowing what to do next.

05 · Engagement strategy

Give users a reason to come back

Tracking alone is not a habit. Nappy uses a light, non-competitive engagement system that rewards consistency and reflection, not perfection.

01

One-tap check-ins

Short forms, saved preferences and optional reminders keep daily logging under one minute.

02

Gentle streaks

Users earn badges for returning, completing a weekly check-in or trying a suggested action. Missing a day never resets their progress.

03

Weekly missions

Small challenges such as “drink water before class” make the goal concrete and connect data to an action.

Reward loop

Progress unlocks visual themes, reflection prompts and optional campus perks - never health claims or public rankings.

Human motivation

Users choose the habit they want to improve, set their own pace and can pause notifications at any time.

06 · What is next with the data?

From data to an action the user can take

Nappy will not just report numbers. It will translate patterns into explainable, low-risk suggestions and let users decide what feels realistic.

1. CollectSleep, water, movement and mood with consent.
2. DetectLook for simple weekly patterns, not diagnoses.
3. Explain“On 4 low-sleep days, your mood was lower.”
4. SuggestOffer one small action matched to the pattern.
5. LearnAsk if it helped and adapt the next goal.
Example: “Your mood was lower on days with less than 6 hours of sleep. Would you like to try a 20-minute wind-down reminder three nights this week?”

Useful output

Weekly summary, pattern cards, one recommended action and links to trusted health resources.

Safety boundary

No diagnosis, treatment or alarmist language. A support screen points users to professional help when appropriate.

07 · Data and technology

A responsible, scalable solution

Data collection

Daily forms for sleep, water, activity and mood, with optional fields and editable entries.

Visualization

Weekly charts, averages, patterns and streaks with accessible text summaries.

Recommendations

Rule-based suggestions in the prototype; later, reviewed content and opt-in personalization with transparent logic.

Privacy

Local or fictional data in the prototype, clear consent, export/delete controls and no sale of personal health data.

Technical principle: start with a simple, auditable recommendation engine. If a future model is added, users should still understand why a suggestion appeared.

4

Possible business model

Freefor students

Core tracking, weekly insights, missions and resource links.

B2Bfor institutions

Universities can sponsor the service and receive only anonymous, aggregated wellbeing trends.

  • Clear consent before every optional data use.

11 · Professor feedback addressed

Observation → correction

We used the feedback to move Nappy from a passive tracker to a small habit-building product.

Observation 01 · Engagement
“Just tracking information and assuming the user would consistently log their information is unrealistic.”

What we changed

Added one-tap check-ins, gentle streaks, weekly missions, self-selected goals, optional reminders and rewards that recognize consistency instead of perfection.

Observation 02 · What is next?
“Once you collect data, how would the user benefit from it? Would you suggest, report or do something else?”

What we changed

Defined a five-step loop: collect, detect patterns, explain the insight, suggest one low-risk action and learn from the user's response.

Observation 03 · Product potential
“How could this be a sustainable app that could eventually become actually a product?”

What we changed

Added a validation-to-beta roadmap, a free student plan, institution-sponsored access, anonymous aggregate dashboards and privacy rules for responsible growth.

Final principle
“The value is not the record. The value is the next helpful decision.”

Result

Nappy now gives users a reason to return, a clear benefit after each check-in and a credible path from class prototype to sustainable service.
